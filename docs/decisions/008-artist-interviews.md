# 008 — Artist interviews become a Backstory show, found by their web address, and they play

**Decision** — Radio Milwaukee's artist interviews (88Nine conversations at `radiomilwaukee.org/discover-music/artist-interviews/…`) are a sixth Backstory show: transcribed, people and places pulled out, approved by an editor, then playable, searchable and quotable through Alexa. Because the station files them in its general "MPX Local Stories" feed rather than a collection of their own, Backstory picks them out by the start of their web address. Alexa plays the interview audio even though the station's content system (CDS, the NPR system the station publishes to) marks it "not streamable".

**Why this came up** — The weekly newsletter's first item on October 1 was the Jeff Levering interview, and the briefing could only offer "Read" for it: Backstory didn't have it. The audio (18 minutes) was sitting in CDS. What was at stake: either a quick shortcut that put unreviewed audio on Alexa, or a slower path that kept the project's one rule, that nothing reaches Alexa until an editor has checked it.

**Options**
- *Play the CDS audio straight from the briefing* — about two hours; but no transcript, no editor review, no search, and it works only from the briefing. The first path to Alexa that skips review.
- *Make interviews a Backstory show, like Ladies First* (chosen) — about a day; every interview transcribed and reviewed, searchable ("what did Peaches say about performance?"), and the briefing links them automatically once published. Costs transcription and AI extraction for each interview, and an editor's time.
- *Leave them as Read links* — no cost, no Play.

**What we chose and why** — The Backstory show (Tarik, 2026-10-05; Claude recommended it). Playing the audio despite the CDS flag was Tarik's call, the same one he made for music premieres: the station already plays these interviews on its own website. Backfill covers 2026 so far (Tarik's choice): 16 interviews, from Robin Hilton in January to Jeff Levering in October. A daily job picks up new ones from the 50 newest stories in the general feed.

**What we gave up** — Interviews the station files under a different web address are missed. The general feed has no show artwork, so each interview uses its own photo. Transcripts misspell names the station's article spells right ("Jake Bowers" for Jake Bauers), and detail answers quote the transcript, so an editor has to catch them. Not every guest is a musician (Levering is a baseball broadcaster), so the extraction instructions have to say so. 16 interviews landed in review at once.

**How we'll know if this was right** — New interviews appear in Needs review each week without anyone importing them; the briefing's interview items show Play once approved; and the reviewed interviews answer detail questions in the guest's own words.
